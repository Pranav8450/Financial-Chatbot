# Insurance — Complete Basics (Term Life & Health)

## The core idea: insurance is protection, not investment

Insurance exists to protect against financial loss from an unlikely but serious event (death, illness, accident) — it is not designed to generate returns the way mutual funds or FDs do. A common beginner mistake is buying insurance-cum-investment products (like traditional endowment or ULIP plans) expecting both good protection and good returns, and often getting mediocre versions of both. The generally recommended approach is to **keep insurance and investment separate**: buy pure protection (term insurance, health insurance) for insurance, and invest separately (SIP, mutual funds, PPF) for wealth building.

## Term life insurance

- **What it is:** you pay a premium, and if you (the policyholder) pass away during the policy term, your nominee receives a large payout (the "sum assured"). If you outlive the term, you generally get nothing back (unless it's a "return of premium" variant, which costs more).
- **Why it's cheap:** since there's no maturity payout in most cases, term insurance is far cheaper than traditional life insurance plans for the same coverage amount — this is precisely why it's usually the recommended way to get life cover.
- **Who needs it:** primarily people with financial dependents — a spouse, children, or parents who rely on their income. If nobody depends on your income, life insurance is generally less urgent.
- **How much cover:** a common rule of thumb is **10-15 times your annual income**, adjusted for outstanding loans (like a home loan) and future obligations (children's education), though this varies by individual circumstances.
- **Buy early:** premiums are locked in based on your age and health at purchase — buying in your 20s-30s while healthy is significantly cheaper than waiting.

## Health insurance

- **What it is:** covers medical treatment costs (hospitalization, surgery, sometimes outpatient treatment) up to a specified sum insured, so a medical emergency doesn't drain your savings or force you into debt.
- **Employer health insurance isn't enough on its own:** it typically ends when you leave the job, and coverage amounts are often modest — most financial advisors recommend having your own independent health policy in addition to any employer-provided one.
- **Family floater vs individual plans:** a family floater covers the whole family under one shared sum insured (cheaper, but the whole family draws from the same pool); individual plans give each person their own dedicated sum insured (costs more, but one person's large claim doesn't reduce cover for others).
- **Waiting periods:** most policies have a waiting period (commonly 2-4 years) before covering pre-existing conditions, and a shorter initial waiting period (30-90 days) before any claims are accepted at all except accidents — this is why buying health insurance early, before you actually need it, matters.

## Tax benefit on health insurance premiums (verified as of September 2026)

- Premiums qualify for a deduction under what was **Section 80D** of the old Income Tax Act, 1961 — renamed **Section 126** under the Income Tax Act, 2025, effective from 1 April 2026. Same rules, new section number.
- **Limits:** up to **₹25,000/year** for premiums covering yourself, spouse, and dependent children (**₹50,000** if any insured member is a senior citizen aged 60+), **plus an additional ₹25,000** for parents' health insurance (**₹50,000** if either parent is a senior citizen) — giving a **maximum combined deduction of ₹1,00,000/year** if both you and your parents are senior citizens.
- A **₹5,000 sub-limit** for preventive health check-ups is included within these limits (and is the one component that can be paid in cash — everything else must be via bank transfer/UPI/card to qualify).
- **This deduction is only available under the old tax regime** — the new (default) tax regime does not allow it.
- As of a GST change in September 2025, individual health insurance premiums attract **nil GST**, so the premium you're quoted is the full amount, with no additional tax on top.

## Common misconceptions

- "I'm young and healthy, I don't need insurance yet" — premiums only get more expensive with age, and health issues that develop later can make coverage harder or costlier to get; buying early locks in low rates while you're insurable.
- "My employer's health cover is enough" — often insufficient in amount and disappears if you change or lose your job.
- "Insurance should also give good returns" — mixing investment into insurance (like ULIPs, endowment plans) usually means paying more for worse versions of both; pure term + pure health, kept separate from investments, is generally more cost-effective.
- "I'll claim health insurance for every small expense" — frequent small claims can affect future premiums/renewal; health insurance is generally best used for genuinely significant medical expenses, not routine minor costs.
