# Real Estate & Property — Complete Basics

## Property as a financial decision, not just a home

Buying property in India is often treated as a default life milestone rather than a deliberate financial decision — worth separating the two lenses: **buying a home to live in** (an emotional/lifestyle decision with financial implications) versus **buying property as an investment** (a financial decision that should be judged like any other investment, on returns and liquidity, not sentiment).

## Rent vs Buy — the actual comparison

This is one of the most common beginner questions, and there's no universal answer — but here's the honest framework:

- **Buying** locks up a large amount of capital (down payment + EMIs) in one illiquid asset, comes with ongoing costs (maintenance, property tax, repairs), and provides stability/ownership.
- **Renting** keeps your capital flexible — that money can instead be invested (SIP, mutual funds), potentially earning more than property appreciation, while renting gives you mobility (easier to relocate for work) and no maintenance responsibility.
- **A common rule of thumb**: if the annual rent for a property is less than roughly 3-4% of that property's purchase price, renting and investing the difference is often the more financially efficient choice — though this varies significantly by city and market conditions, and doesn't account for the non-financial value of owning a home.
- **This is not purely financial for most people** — stability, not dealing with landlords, and the psychological value of ownership are legitimate factors beyond the math, and it's reasonable for someone to choose buying even if renting is technically the better financial trade, or vice versa.

## Costs beyond the property price (often underestimated by first-time buyers)

- **Stamp duty and registration charges** — vary by state, commonly in the range of 5-8% of property value combined, a significant one-time cost often not budgeted for upfront.
- **Brokerage** — typically 1-2% of the transaction value if using an agent.
- **GST** — applicable on under-construction properties (not on ready-to-move properties with a completion certificate), which is an important distinction when comparing prices.
- **Maintenance charges** — ongoing monthly/annual costs for society/building upkeep, separate from the loan EMI.
- **Home loan processing fees** — a percentage of the loan amount charged by the bank upfront.

## Key documents and due diligence for buyers

- **Title deed** — proof of the seller's legal ownership; verifying a clean title (no disputes, no pending litigation) is critical before purchase.
- **Encumbrance certificate** — confirms the property has no outstanding loans or legal dues attached to it.
- **RERA registration** — under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, most new residential/commercial projects must be registered with the state's RERA authority, which provides some protection against project delays and misrepresentation. Checking a project's RERA registration status is a standard due-diligence step in most states today.
- **Occupancy/Completion certificate** — confirms the building is legally approved for occupation, relevant when buying ready or nearly-ready properties.

## Real estate as an investment (beyond a primary home)

- **Illiquidity** is the biggest structural difference from stocks/mutual funds — selling property can take months, unlike redeeming a mutual fund in a day or two.
- **Rental yield** in most major Indian cities is relatively low (commonly in the 2-4% range annually, before expenses), meaning property investment returns have historically depended more on price appreciation than rental income — this varies significantly by city and property type.
- **REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)** offer an alternative way to get real estate exposure without buying physical property — you buy units (similar to a stock) in a fund that owns and manages commercial real estate (like office buildings), earning a share of rental income and potential price appreciation, with much better liquidity than physical property since REIT units trade on stock exchanges.

## Home loan basics recap (see the Loans article for full detail)

- Interest paid is deductible up to ₹2 lakh/year under Section 24(b) for a self-occupied property (old tax regime only).
- Principal repayment counts toward the shared ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C/123 limit.
- Most home loans in India are floating rate, and RBI rules generally prohibit prepayment penalties on floating-rate loans for individual borrowers.

## Common misconceptions

- "Property always appreciates" — not guaranteed; property values can stagnate or decline in specific markets/periods, same as any asset class, and are also affected heavily by location-specific factors.
- "Buying is always better than renting" — depends heavily on the specific numbers (rent vs EMI, opportunity cost of capital, how long you'll stay) — not a universal truth.
- "Under-construction properties are always cheaper" — often true at listing price, but come with construction-delay risk, GST that ready properties don't have, and the property doesn't generate rent/utility until completed.
- "REITs are the same as buying property directly" — REITs give indirect, liquid exposure to commercial real estate income; they're a genuinely different investment vehicle from owning physical property, with different risk, liquidity, and control characteristics.
