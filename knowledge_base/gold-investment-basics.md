# Gold as an Investment — Complete Basics

## Why people invest in gold

Gold has traditionally played two roles in Indian household finance: a cultural/ceremonial asset (jewelry, gifting) and a financial hedge — something that has historically tended to hold or gain value during periods of economic uncertainty, currency weakness, or high inflation, and generally moves somewhat independently of the stock market. This is why gold is often suggested as a small diversification component (commonly 5-10% of a portfolio) rather than a primary investment.

## Ways to invest in gold

### Physical gold (jewelry, coins, bars)
- Most familiar and culturally significant form, but has real downsides as an investment: **making charges** (for jewelry, typically 8-25% of value, non-recoverable when you sell), **storage/security concerns**, **purity verification issues**, and **no additional income** (unlike gold bonds, physical gold doesn't pay interest).
- Best suited for genuine personal/cultural use rather than as a primary investment vehicle.

### Digital gold
- Lets you buy small amounts of gold online (even ₹1 worth), held in insured vaults by the provider, without physically storing anything yourself.
- **Important caution:** digital gold is **not directly regulated by SEBI or RBI** the way mutual funds or gold ETFs are — it operates through private platforms, which has drawn regulatory scrutiny in recent years. This is worth knowing and flagging clearly if a user asks about it, rather than presenting it as equally regulated as other gold investment routes.

### Gold ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)
- A mutual fund-like product that trades on the stock exchange, backed by physical gold held by the fund. You buy/sell units through a demat account, just like a stock.
- Regulated by SEBI, generally low-cost (expense ratio), and highly liquid (tradeable any market day) — a common recommended route for beginners wanting gold exposure without physical storage concerns.

### Gold mutual funds (Fund of Funds)
- Invest in gold ETFs on your behalf — useful if you want to do a SIP into gold without needing a demat account, since these can be bought the same way as any other mutual fund.
- Slightly higher expense ratio than buying a gold ETF directly, since there's an extra layer of fund management.

### Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB) — important current status (verified as of September 2026)

- SGBs were a government-issued bond linked to gold prices, paying a small additional interest (2.5% per year) on top of gold's price movement, with tax-free capital gains at maturity for original subscribers — historically one of the most tax-efficient ways to hold gold.
- **The scheme has been discontinued for new issuances since February 2024** — no new SGB tranche has been issued since then, and no issuance calendar has been announced for FY 2026-27. The Finance Ministry has confirmed this discontinuation is intentional, citing the high cost of the redemption liability to the government.
- **If you already hold SGBs:** they remain valid and continue paying interest until maturity (8 years from issuance) or eligible premature redemption (generally after 5 years, on specific interest payment dates).
- **You can still buy existing SGBs on the secondary market** (NSE/BSE) through a demat account, but **the tax treatment changed from 1 April 2026**: the capital-gains tax exemption at maturity now applies only to the *original* subscriber who holds to full 8-year maturity. If you buy an SGB on the secondary market, you'll pay capital gains tax on your gain — 12.5% LTCG if held over 12 months, or slab-rate STCG if sold within 12 months — same as other listed securities. The 2.5% annual interest remains taxable at your slab rate for all holders, regardless of when purchased.
- **Practical implication for a beginner asking about gold bonds today:** SGBs are not a "buy new" option anymore — direct new investors should generally look at gold ETFs or gold mutual funds instead, and should not expect to find a fresh SGB subscription window.

## How gold gains are taxed (non-SGB routes)

- **Physical gold, gold ETFs, and gold mutual funds**: held over 12 months qualifies as long-term capital gains, generally taxed similarly to other capital assets at 12.5% (post the 2024 tax rule simplification that aligned most asset classes); held 12 months or less is short-term, taxed at your income slab rate.
- **Digital gold**: taxation generally follows a similar capital gains framework, though as an unregulated category, users should be cautious about assuming complete consistency with regulated instruments.

## Common misconceptions

- "Gold always goes up" — false; gold prices do fluctuate and can decline over shorter periods, even though it has a strong long-term track record as a store of value.
- "I can still buy new Sovereign Gold Bonds directly from the government" — false as of the current status described above; this is one of the most-changed facts in personal finance content recently, so worth double-checking if referencing older articles.
- "Physical gold and gold ETFs are taxed the same as SGBs" — not for original SGB subscribers, who get a tax-free maturity benefit not available with any other gold route.
- "Digital gold is just as regulated as gold ETFs" — false; digital gold sits outside SEBI/RBI's direct regulatory framework in the way ETFs and mutual funds do, which is a meaningful difference in investor protection.
