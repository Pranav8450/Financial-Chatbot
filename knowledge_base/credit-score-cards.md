# Credit Score & Credit Cards — Complete Basics

## What is a credit score?

A credit score is a three-digit number (in India, most commonly the **CIBIL score**, ranging from **300 to 900**) that represents how reliably you've repaid borrowed money in the past. Banks and lenders use it to decide whether to approve a loan/credit card application, and at what interest rate. A higher score signals lower risk to the lender.

- **750+** is generally considered a good/strong score that gets favorable loan terms.
- **Below 650** typically makes approvals harder or comes with higher interest rates.
- Other bureaus also exist in India (Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark), but CIBIL is the most commonly referenced.

## What affects your credit score

- **Repayment history (the biggest factor)** — paying credit card bills and loan EMIs on time, every time. Even a single missed payment can meaningfully hurt your score.
- **Credit utilization** — how much of your total available credit card limit you're actually using. Keeping this well below 30% of your limit (rather than maxing out cards) generally helps your score.
- **Credit mix** — having a mix of secured (like a home/car loan) and unsecured credit (credit cards, personal loans) can help, though this matters less than payment history.
- **Length of credit history** — older, well-managed accounts generally help your score; closing your oldest credit card can sometimes reduce your score by shortening your average credit history.
- **Frequency of new credit applications** — applying for many loans/cards in a short period can lower your score, since each hard inquiry has a small negative impact and multiple applications can signal financial stress to lenders.

## How to check your credit score

You're entitled to **one free credit report per bureau per year** directly from CIBIL, Experian, Equifax, and CRIF High Mark. Many banking apps and financial apps also show your score for free (sometimes updated monthly), often as a way to cross-sell loan/card products — the score itself is free to check regardless of where you check it.

## How credit cards actually work

1. You get a credit limit — the maximum you can spend on the card.
2. You make purchases during a billing cycle (typically ~30 days).
3. At the end of the cycle, a bill is generated for everything you spent.
4. You get an interest-free period (commonly 20-50 days total from purchase date, depending on when in the cycle you bought something) to pay the bill in full with **no interest charged**.
5. If you pay the full bill by the due date, you pay zero interest — the "free credit" only stays free if paid in full and on time.

## The most important thing beginners get wrong: minimum due amount

Every credit card bill shows a **"minimum amount due"** (often just 5% of the total bill) alongside the **total amount due**. Paying only the minimum keeps your account "in good standing" on the surface, but:
- **Interest is charged on the entire outstanding balance**, not just the unpaid portion, and credit card interest rates are very high — commonly **36%-46% per year** (roughly 3%-4% per month) in India.
- New purchases made after that stop getting the interest-free period until the full previous balance is cleared.

This is how credit card debt spirals for many beginners — paying only the minimum feels manageable each month but the interest compounds fast. **The single most important habit: always pay the full statement amount, every month, not just the minimum.**

## Benefits of using a credit card responsibly

- Builds your credit history/score, which helps when you apply for bigger loans later (home loan, car loan) — someone with no credit history at all can actually find it harder to get approved than someone with a well-managed history.
- Reward points, cashback, and purchase protection benefits, depending on the card.
- Emergency purchasing power without needing cash on hand.

## Common misconceptions

- "Carrying a small balance helps my credit score" — false; this is a myth. Paying in full every month is what helps your score; carrying a balance just costs you high interest with no score benefit.
- "Having a credit card is bad/risky" — the card itself isn't the problem; not paying the full bill on time is. Used responsibly, it's a useful financial tool.
- "Checking my own credit score hurts it" — false; checking your own score ("soft inquiry") has no negative effect. Only when a lender checks it as part of a loan/card application ("hard inquiry") does it have a small impact.
- "More credit cards always looks better" — not necessarily; what matters is responsible use and on-time payment, not simply the number of cards held.
