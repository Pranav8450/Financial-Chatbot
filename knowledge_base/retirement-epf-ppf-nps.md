# Retirement Planning — EPF, PPF & NPS Complete Basics

## Why retirement planning needs its own approach

Retirement is a goal decades away for most beginners, but it needs deliberate planning specifically because compounding needs a long time horizon to work powerfully, and because these government-backed retirement instruments each have distinct rules, lock-ins, and tax treatment worth understanding — they're often confused with each other or with regular mutual fund SIPs.

## EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)

- **What it is:** a mandatory retirement savings scheme for salaried employees at companies above a certain size — both you and your employer contribute a percentage of your basic salary every month (commonly 12% each), managed by the EPFO (Employees' Provident Fund Organisation).
- **You don't choose to opt in/out** — if you're a salaried employee at an eligible company, this is typically automatic, deducted directly from your salary.
- **Interest rate:** government-notified annually, revised periodically (currently in the 8%+ per year range as of recent years — this rate is reviewed and can change, so check the current EPFO-notified rate rather than treating any single figure as fixed long-term).
- **Tax treatment:** contributions qualify under Section 80C/123 (within the shared ₹1.5 lakh limit), and interest earned is tax-free up to a threshold — interest on contributions above ₹2.5 lakh/year (₹5 lakh/year for cases with no employer contribution) is taxable, a rule introduced to limit the tax-free benefit for very high contributors.
- **Withdrawal:** meant to stay untouched until retirement (age 58) for maximum benefit; early withdrawal is allowed under specific circumstances (job loss, medical emergency, home purchase) but generally reduces the long-term compounding benefit. Switching jobs should involve transferring your EPF via UAN (Universal Account Number) rather than withdrawing it, to preserve continuity.

## PPF (Public Provident Fund)

- **What it is:** a voluntary, government-backed long-term savings scheme open to any Indian resident (not tied to employment) — you can open one at a bank or post office.
- **Interest rate (verified as of September 2026):** **7.1% per annum**, reviewed quarterly by the government, though it has remained unchanged since April 2020.
- **Lock-in:** **15 years**, though partial withdrawals are allowed from the 7th year onward under specific conditions, and loans against the balance are available in certain years.
- **Tax treatment — EEE status:** PPF has the most favorable tax treatment of any common instrument — **E**xempt on contribution (under Section 80C/123, old regime only), **E**xempt on interest earned (no tax annually, in any regime), and **E**xempt at maturity/withdrawal (fully tax-free). Under the new tax regime, you don't get the contribution deduction, but the interest and maturity amount remain completely tax-free regardless of regime.
- **Contribution limit:** up to ₹1.5 lakh per year counts toward the Section 80C/123 deduction (you can technically deposit more in some cases, but only ₹1.5 lakh gets the tax benefit).
- **Timing tip:** interest is calculated on the lowest balance between the 5th and last day of each month — so depositing before the 5th of the month (rather than later) means that month's deposit starts earning interest immediately instead of missing that month.

## NPS (National Pension System)

- **What it is:** a market-linked, government-regulated pension scheme where your contributions are invested in a mix of equity, corporate bonds, and government securities (you can typically choose the mix, within limits), aiming for higher growth potential than fixed-return options like PPF.
- **Tier 1 vs Tier 2:** Tier 1 is the actual retirement account with tax benefits and a lock-in until retirement age; Tier 2 is a more flexible, no-lock-in account but comes with **no special tax benefit** — it's essentially a regular investment account with NPS's fee structure, and many advisors suggest using mutual funds instead of Tier 2 for flexible investing.
- **Tax benefits (verified as of September 2026):**
  - Regular contributions qualify under Section 80C/123 (shared ₹1.5 lakh limit), old regime only.
  - **Section 80CCD(1B)** offers an **additional ₹50,000 deduction**, exclusively for NPS Tier 1 contributions, over and above the ₹1.5 lakh 80C/123 limit — this is a genuinely distinct extra benefit not available through EPF, PPF, or ELSS.
  - **Section 80CCD(2)** covers employer contributions to your NPS — and uniquely, this benefit is available under **both the old and new tax regimes**. As of FY 2025-26 onward, employers can contribute up to **14% of basic salary** (raised from the earlier 10% for private-sector employees) entirely tax-free in the employee's hands.
- **At retirement (age 60):** up to **60% of the accumulated corpus can be withdrawn tax-free as a lump sum**; the remaining **40% must go into an annuity** (a plan that pays you a regular pension), and those monthly annuity payouts are taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate when received.

## Quick comparison: which does what

- **EPF** — your automatic, employer-linked foundation if you're salaried; you generally can't opt out, and shouldn't try to.
- **PPF** — your voluntary, fully guaranteed, completely tax-free layer; best for anyone (including self-employed people without EPF access) wanting zero-risk, tax-free long-term savings.
- **NPS** — your growth-oriented layer with the highest tax efficiency per rupee invested (especially via the extra ₹50,000 80CCD(1B) deduction and the employer 80CCD(2) benefit), at the cost of market-linked risk and a mandatory annuity portion at exit.

Most financial advisors suggest using a combination of all three where accessible, rather than treating them as competing alternatives — they serve different roles in a retirement plan.

## Common misconceptions

- "I don't need to think about retirement in my 20s/30s" — the biggest advantage of PPF/NPS/EPF is compounding over a long horizon; starting a decade later can mean a meaningfully smaller final corpus even with higher later contributions.
- "NPS Tier 2 has the same tax benefit as Tier 1" — false, only Tier 1 gets tax deductions.
- "Withdrawing EPF when I switch jobs is fine" — technically possible under some conditions, but breaks the compounding and continuity; transferring via UAN is generally the better move.
- "PPF and NPS 80C benefit stack separately" — false, PPF, EPF, ELSS, and NPS's regular 80CCD(1) contribution all share the *same* ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C/123 cap; only NPS's extra ₹50,000 (80CCD(1B)) and employer contributions (80CCD(2)) sit outside that shared cap.
